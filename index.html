How it works

  1. Host a game

    Pick a format like tag or a scavenger hunt, then set the place, the time and the rules.

  2. Players join

    Followers get the alert and people nearby can jump in. Every player films their own view.

  3. Play it live

    Viewers vote on what happens next, and the crowd or the host calls the result.

Watching counts too. Viewers vote while it happens, back the players they like, and can throw in a new twist in the middle of a game.

Ways to play

A few games hosts can run. Tap one to see how a round goes.

Make your place the finish line.

For cafés, restaurants, stores and venues. A host pins your location, people nearby race to get there, and the whole thing streams live.

Code word rush

A host pins your café. People within half a mile have 15 minutes to get there and say the code word to the barista. The first 10 are in; everyone else watches.

  1. The host pins your café on the map
  2. The zone locks to half a mile
  3. A 15-minute countdown starts
  4. The first 10 to say the word are in
"Get to the café on 4th St and tell the barista the code word before the timer runs out. Ten spots."

Wing showdown

Team up with a host for a live eating contest. Viewers vote on the heat level and players race the clock.

  1. Your restaurant goes live with a host
  2. Players take their seats
  3. Viewers pick mild, hot or extreme
  4. Six wings before the buzzer
"Six wings, three minutes, and the crowd picked the hottest sauce on the menu."

Style battle

Your store floor becomes the stage. Players build a full outfit under budget in five minutes, and viewers vote on the best look.

  1. Your store goes live
  2. Players pick items under $50
  3. Five minutes to put a look together
  4. Viewers pick the winner
"Head-to-toe look, under $50, in five minutes. The crowd decides who wins."

First through the door

Drop a live pin and people nearby race to your door. The first five get pulled into a live game the viewers choose.

  1. Your venue drops a pin
  2. One-mile zone, and the clock starts
  3. The first five through the door are in
  4. Viewers pick the game
"First five through the door sing karaoke live. The crowd picks the song."

Questions

Is Ultimatum free?

Yes. Watching, browsing and joining games are free. There's no entry fee and no subscription.

How do hosts and players make money?

Through optional support from viewers. Viewers can send support during a game, but it's never required to watch or play.

Is this gambling?

No. There's no betting, no entry fees and no prize pools. Votes decide how a game plays out, not who wins money.

What isn't allowed?

Pornography, explicit content, gambling and anything illegal. Anyone can report a video, and we review reports and remove anything that breaks the rules.

How does voting work?

While a game is live, viewers vote on what happens next and whether the player passed or failed. Votes count in real time.

Can my business use Ultimatum?

Yes. A café, restaurant, store or venue can be the location for a game, which brings nearby people through your door. Email us to set one up.

Scavenger hunt

The host sets the location to downtown, locks the zone to half a mile, and gives players 30 minutes.

How a round goes

  1. Host: "Find the mural on 5th Ave and take a photo with it."
  2. Vote: viewers pick where the next clue is.
  3. Host: "Run to the fountain and say the code phrase to the person in the red hat."
  4. Vote: did they make it? Pass or fail.

What the host sets

Location
Any spot on the map
Time limit
How long players have
Votes
What the crowd gets to decide
Support
Viewers can back the players they like

What viewers do

Vote on which clue drops next and whether each player passed or failed, and send support to the players you're rooting for.

"Find the gold envelope hidden near the old market. The crowd picks your next clue in 60 seconds."

Crowd's choice

A host goes live in a busy park with three options ready. Whatever the crowd picks, they do on the spot. Then the next round starts.

How a round goes

  1. Host: posts three options.
  2. Vote: "Sing opera to a stranger" wins.
  3. Host: does it, live.
  4. Vote: pass or fail, then three new options.

What the host sets

Options
Anything they're willing to do
Location
Where they go live
Time limit
How long each one gets
Support
Viewers can back the host

What viewers do

Pick what they do. The host doesn't know which option wins until voting closes. After each one, you vote pass or fail.

"Option A: sing opera to a stranger. Option B: ask someone to slow dance. Option C: give a one-minute pep talk from a park bench. Voting is open."

Hide-and-seek

Two players, a four-block zone and a 20-minute clock. Viewers can switch between both players' cameras the whole time.

How a round goes

  1. Host: "Hider, you have 3 minutes to disappear."
  2. Vote: should the seeker get a hint?
  3. Host: "Seeker, check the alley on 3rd Ave." (the hint the crowd unlocked)
  4. Vote: found in time? The crowd decides.

What the host sets

Zone
Where the game can go
Time limit
How long the chase runs
Votes
Hints and the final call
Support
Viewers can back either player

What viewers do

Watch both sides, vote on hints and the final call, and back the hider or the seeker.

"The seeker is two blocks away and the hider just ducked into an alley. Reveal the hider's street? Vote now."

City tag

Four players, a two-mile zone, and one player is it. The host sets the rules, the area and the time.

How a round goes

  1. Host: "Everyone scatter. The tagger is released in 2 minutes."
  2. Vote: where should a safe zone drop?
  3. Host: a safe zone opens on Main St for 3 minutes.
  4. Vote: Player 3 got tagged. Was it clean?

What the host sets

Zone
The full play area
Time limit
How long the game runs
Votes
Safe zones and disputed tags
Support
Each player's fans can back them

What viewers do

Back your player, vote on where safe zones appear, and settle disputed tags.

"The tagger is closing in on Player 3, but the crowd just dropped a safe zone on Main St. Will they make it?"
